# Fee Mitigation

A merchant pays a cost to accept a card. **Fee mitigation** lets the merchant move part of that
cost to the customer, or reward a customer who pays another way.

Handpoint supports four programs. A transaction carries **one** program. It never carries two.

## The four programs

| Program | What the customer sees | Tenders |
|---|---|---|
| **Surcharge** | The price, plus a separate fee for the use of a credit card | Credit cards only |
| **Dual pricing** | Two prices: a cash price, and a higher card price | Every card |
| **Cash discount** | The card price, and a lower price if they pay cash | Cash and e-gift only |
| **Admin fee** | The price, plus a fee that applies to every payment method | Every tender |

### Why the difference matters

The difference between these programs is legal, not arithmetic. Two programs can charge the same
money and follow different rules.

- A **surcharge** is a fee. The card networks regulate it, and several US states prohibit it.
- **Dual pricing** is a fee that the gateway applies when the conditions of the program are met.
  The merchant posts two prices: a cash price and a higher card price.
- A **cash discount** reduces the price when the customer pays cash. The card price is the standard
  price.
- An **admin fee** applies to every payment method. It is deliberately universal. A carve-out for
  cash would turn it into a cash discount, and the rules would change.

## Who calculates the fee

**You calculate the fee. The gateway decides whether it applies.**

Only your application knows the merchant configuration, the tax rate, and the rounding you printed
on the customer's screen. So you send the amount you calculated, and the gateway never recalculates
it.

The gateway then makes one decision: does this program apply to this card? If it does not, the
gateway removes the fee and tells you why.

```
Your application            The gateway
─────────────────           ───────────
calculate the fee    ──▶    check the card type
                            apply, or drop and explain
show the result      ◀──    return the outcome
```

## What the platform guarantees

- The fee is calculated **before** the authorization.
- The final transaction amount includes the fee.
- A debit card never carries a surcharge.
- The merchant configures the rules, and the platform enforces them.
- The customer sees the final price before they confirm the payment.

## Eligibility

Two facts decide the outcome: the type of the card, and the type of the transaction.

### Cards

| Program | Cards |
|---|---|
| Surcharge | Credit only. Never debit, never prepaid |
| Dual pricing | Every card |
| Admin fee | Every card |
| Cash discount | None. The gateway refuses it |

**The gateway refuses a cash discount on a card transaction.** A card transaction under a cash
discount program carries no fee, because the card price is the posted price. Such a request cannot
be correct, so the gateway rejects it rather than accepting it quietly.

:::note A cash discount is rejected on a card
The gateway returns HTTP `400` with error `4272` (`FEE_PROGRAM_NOT_SUPPORTED`). Apply a cash discount
in your own application, on a cash or e-gift tender only.
:::

### Dropped and refused are different

| Outcome | What happens | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Dropped** | The transaction continues. The gateway removes the fee, and the result says why | A surcharge on a debit card |
| **Refused** | The transaction never reaches the acquirer. The gateway returns an error | A fee that conflicts with `surchargeAmount` |

Always read the result. A dropped fee means the customer paid less than you asked for, and your
receipt and your records must show the amount the gateway actually charged.

### Transaction types

| Transaction type | Surcharge | Dual pricing | Admin fee | Cash discount |
|---|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| Sale | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ❌ |
| Sale with tip | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ❌ |
| Sale with tokenization | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ❌ |
| Remote Sale | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ❌ |
| Pre-Authorization, create | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ |
| Pre-Authorization, capture | ✅ | ❌ | ✅ | ❌ |
| Remote Pre-Authorization, capture | ✅ | ❌ | ✅ | ❌ |
| Refund | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ |
| Remote Refund | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ |
| Reversal | Automatic | Automatic | Automatic | ❌ |
| Capture reversal | Automatic | Automatic | Automatic | ❌ |
| Tip adjustment | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ |
| Tokenization | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ |
| Batch operations | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ |

Four rules explain every ❌ in that table.

**A pre-authorization carries no fee.** The fee belongs to the capture, because the capture sets the
amount the customer pays. If you set a fee on a pre-authorization, the SDK removes it. Set it on the
capture instead.

**Dual pricing needs a cash price.** A pre-authorization has no cash alternative to price against, so
dual pricing applies to a sale and to a Remote Sale only.

**A refund carries no fee.** The card networks require a merchant to return the fee in proportion to
the refund. The platform does not calculate that proportion, so you calculate it. Read the note
below.

**An operation with no amount carries no fee.** Tokenization, a tip adjustment and every batch
operation set no transaction amount, so no fee can apply to them.

**A reversal is automatic.** The reversal carries the same fee as the original transaction, because
the acquirer requires the two values to match. You set nothing.

:::warning A refund does not return the fee for you
Set no fee on a refund. From Android SDK 7.1015.0, the SDK does not start a refund with a fee. See
[Surcharging — Refunds](/reference/surcharging#refunds).

Calculate the returned fee in your own application, and add it to the refund amount you send. A
refund that does not return the fee in proportion breaks card network rules, and the merchant
carries that risk.
:::

## How the Android SDK builds the amount {#amount}

Send the **base amount**. The Android SDK adds the tip, the fee and the tax:

```
amount = base + tip + fee.amount + tax
```

- The rule applies to a sale on every entry mode (chip, contactless and swipe) and to a Remote Sale,
  which has no tip. The card amount (EMV tag `9F02`) is the same value.
- `tax` is `taxInformation.taxAmount`, or `0` when `taxExempt` is `true`. The SDK uses the deprecated
  `taxAmount` only when `taxInformation` is not set.
- A pre-authorization carries no fee and no tax. Its capture uses the rule above.

:::note Coming soon
The capture rule comes with Android SDK 7.1015.0. Until then, the SDK captures the amount that you
send, and sends the fee and the tax as fields only.
:::

:::warning Do not put the tax in `amount`
Before Android SDK 7.1014.5, the SDK did not add `taxInformation.taxAmount`. If your integration puts
the tax in `amount`, the customer pays it two times.
:::

`POST /transactions` does not accept a `fee` object yet. When it does, it accepts a nested `fee`
object for `sale`, `moToSale`, `saleAndTokenizeCard` and `preAuthorizationCapture`, in the shape
below. The `amount` of the request is the base amount. The terminal adds the tip, the fee and the

```json
{
  "operation": "sale",
  "amount": "10000",
  "currency": "USD",
  "terminal_type": "PAXA920",
  "serial_number": "082104578",
  "fee": {
    "amount": "300",
    "mitigationProgram": "surcharge",
    "taxOnFee": "60"
  },
  "taxInformation": {
    "taxAmount": "2060",
    "taxExempt": false
  },
  "purchaseOrderNumber": "PO42"
}
```

`amount` is the base amount, `10000` (100.00). The fee is `300` (3.00) without its tax. The tax on the
fee, `60`, is separate, and `taxInformation.taxAmount` includes it. The terminal charges
`10000 + 300 + 2060 = 12360`.

Amounts inside `fee` follow the rest of `POST /transactions`: minor units, as a string.

| Field | Type | Required | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| `amount` | string | Yes | The fee, without the tax on it. Digits only, 12 digits at most |
| `mitigationProgram` | string | Yes | `surcharge`, `adminFee` or `dualPricing`. The match is exact |
| `taxOnFee` | string | No | The tax on the fee. Digits only. It is part of `taxInformation.taxAmount` |

The Cloud API checks only the format, the program and the operation. It returns HTTP `400` for an
unknown program, `cashDiscount`, `dualPricing` on `preAuthorizationCapture`, an amount that is not
digits only, or a `fee` on another operation. The terminal
applies the other rules in this page, for example `taxOnFee` not above `taxInformation.taxAmount`.

:::note The old `surchargeAmount` is deprecated
`surchargeAmount` is deprecated in favour of `fee`. It is a surcharge that the terminal adds to the
base `amount`. It is not already included in `amount`.
:::

Build a `Fee`, set it on the options, and start the operation. Pass the **base amount** to
`hapi.sale()`. The SDK adds `fee.amount` and the tax amount to it, so never add them yourself.
The `amount` of a request is always the base amount.

```kotlin
val fee = Fee(
    amount            = BigInteger("300"),   // 3.00, without tax
    mitigationProgram = FeeMitigationProgram.SURCHARGE,
    taxOnFee          = BigInteger("60")     // 0.60, part of the tax amount
)

val options = SaleOptions()
options.fee = fee

hapi.sale(BigInteger("10000"), Currency.USD, options)   // 100.00

override fun endOfTransaction(result: TransactionResult, device: Device) {
    if (result.finStatus == FinancialStatus.AUTHORISED) {
        val feeResult = result.fee ?: return
        if (feeResult.applied) {
            // a surcharge prints its own fee line; settle result.totalAmount
        } else {
            // no fee line — read feeResult.reason
        }
    }
}
```

A Remote Sale uses the same object on `MoToOptions`:

```kotlin
val options = MoToOptions()
options.fee = Fee(BigInteger("300"), FeeMitigationProgram.SURCHARGE, BigInteger("60"))

hapi.moToSale(BigInteger("10000"), Currency.USD, options)
```

:::note The old `surchargeAmount` still works
`Options.surchargeAmount` and `TransactionResult.surcharge` are deprecated, and they keep working.
The SDK reads `surchargeAmount` as `Fee(amount, SURCHARGE)` when you set no `fee`, and it fills
`TransactionResult.surcharge` as well as `TransactionResult.fee` for a surcharge. Set one field or
the other. If you set both, the two amounts must match, and the program must be `SURCHARGE`.
:::

## Fee fields {#fee-fields}

The object you send.

| Name | Type | Required | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| `amount` | integer, minor units | Yes | The fee without its tax. The SDK adds it to the base amount of the operation. Never negative |
| `mitigationProgram` | enum | Yes | `SURCHARGE`, `ADMIN_FEE`, `CASH_DISCOUNT` or `DUAL_PRICING`. On the wire: `surcharge`, `adminFee`, `cashDiscount`, `dualPricing` |
| `taxOnFee` | integer, minor units | No | The part of the tax amount that is tax on the fee. It is not part of `amount`. Never negative. Absent reads as zero |

Three rules apply before the transaction starts.

1. `amount` and `taxOnFee` must not be negative.
2. If you set `fee` and the deprecated `surchargeAmount` together, the two amounts must match, and
   `mitigationProgram` must be `SURCHARGE`.
3. `taxOnFee` must not exceed `taxInformation.taxAmount`, when you set `taxInformation`.

## What comes back {#what-comes-back}

Every transaction that carried a fee returns a fee result. A transaction that carried none returns
nothing, not a zero.

| Name | Type | Required | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| `amount` | decimal, major units | Yes | The fee the gateway recorded. When `applied` is `false`, this is what you asked for, not what the customer paid |
| `mitigationProgram` | enum | No | The program the gateway applied the fee under. Empty when the gateway reports a program the SDK does not recognise |
| `taxOnFee` | decimal, major units | Yes | The part of the tax amount that is tax on the fee. It is not part of `amount` |
| `applied` | boolean | Yes | Whether the gateway charged the fee |
| `reason` | enum | Yes | Why the gateway applied or dropped the fee |
| `reasonDetail` | string | No | The raw text from the gateway. Set when `reason` is `UNKNOWN` |

**The amount you send and the amount you read use different units.** You send minor units. You read
major units, the same as every other amount on the result.

### Why the fee was applied or dropped {#reasons}

| Name | Meaning | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| `APPLIED` | The gateway charged the fee | Print the fee line. Settle the total that the result carries |
| `NOT_ELIGIBLE_DEBIT` | The customer presented a debit card, and the program does not allow one | Print no fee line. Tell the customer the fee was removed |
| `NOT_ELIGIBLE_PREPAID` | The customer presented a prepaid card, and the program does not allow one | Print no fee line. Tell the customer the fee was removed |
| `FEE_AMOUNT_MISSING` | The fee had a `taxOnFee` but no `amount` | Print no fee line. Send `fee.amount` |
| `PROGRAM_NOT_ENABLED` | The merchant is not configured for this program | Print no fee line. Ask the merchant to enable the program |
| `PROGRAM_NOT_SUPPORTED` | The gateway does not carry this program | Print no fee line. Handle the program in your own application |
| `UNKNOWN` | The gateway returned a reason this version of the SDK does not recognise | Read `reasonDetail`. Treat the fee as dropped unless `applied` is `true` |

## Errors

The gateway refuses these requests before the authorization. The transaction never reaches the
acquirer.

| Code | Meaning | Recovery |
|---|---|---|
| `4258` `SURCHARGE_NOT_ENABLED` | The merchant is not configured to surcharge, and the request carries a surcharge | Ask the merchant to enable surcharging, or send no fee |
| `4268` `FEE_SURCHARGE_AMOUNT_CONFLICT` | The request carries both `fee` and the deprecated `surchargeAmount`, and they disagree | Send one field or the other. If you send both, match the amounts and set `mitigationProgram` to `surcharge` |
| `4269` `FEE_TAX_ON_FEE_EXCEEDS_L2_TAX` | `fee.taxOnFee` is greater than `taxInformation.taxAmount` | Lower `taxOnFee`, or raise the tax you declare |
| `FIELD_REQUIRED` | `fee.amount` or `fee.mitigationProgram` is missing | Send both fields. Neither one is optional |
| `AMOUNT_FORMAT` | `fee.amount` or `fee.taxOnFee` is malformed or negative | Send a positive amount in the documented format |
| `MITIGATION_PROGRAM_INVALID` | `fee.mitigationProgram` is not one of the four values | Send `surcharge`, `adminFee`, `cashDiscount` or `dualPricing` |

:::warning A half-migrated integration fails loudly, on purpose
The gateway refuses a request that carries two different fee amounts. It never picks one for you.
Migrate a call site completely, in one change, from `surchargeAmount` to `fee`.
:::

The Android SDK checks the same three rules before it sends, and it raises a verification error
instead of a transaction. See [Error handling](error-handling-guide.md).

## Tax on the fee

A merchant can calculate the fee before the tax, or after it. Both orders reach the same total.
They do not reach the same tax.

`taxOnFee` names the part of the tax amount that is tax on the fee. It is not part of
`fee.amount`. The gateway rejects a `taxOnFee` that is greater than the Level 2 tax, and when it
drops a fee, it removes both `fee.amount` and `fee.taxOnFee` from the transaction amount.

Example. The base is 100.00. The tax is 20%, so the tax on the base is 20.00. The merchant
surcharges 3% of the base, and the merchant configuration applies tax to the surcharge as well.

| Value | Amount | What you send |
|---|---|---|
| Base | 100.00 | The amount of the operation, `10000` |
| Fee on the base at 3% | 3.00 | `fee.amount`, `300` |
| Tax on the fee at 20% | 0.60 | `fee.taxOnFee`, `60` |
| Tax amount, with the tax on the fee | 20.60 | `taxInformation.taxAmount`, `2060` |
| What the customer pays | 123.60 | The SDK adds the base, the fee and the tax amount |

You send the tax on the fee, because only you know how you calculated it. Include it in the tax
amount that you send.

:::note The tax on a dropped fee
When the gateway drops a fee, it removes the fee. The matching reduction of the declared tax is
still in development. Until it ships, reconcile the tax yourself when `applied` is `false`.
:::

## What reaches the card networks

**Only a surcharge.** The acquirer protocol has one field for a fee, and its specification calls
that field the surcharge. It has no representation for the other three programs.

An admin fee and a dual pricing fee travel inside the transaction amount, and nothing else.
Reporting either one as a surcharge would misstate a fee to the card networks.

## The receipt

The receipt is the only record a customer keeps, and the only one a regulator inspects. Each
program has its own rule.

| Program | The receipt shows |
|---|---|
| **Surcharge** | A separate line, clearly labelled. The card networks **require** it. Never inside the total, never mixed with the tax |
| **Admin fee** | No fee line. The fee is included in the amount line |
| **Dual pricing** | No fee line. The fee is included in the amount line |
| **Cash discount** | Nothing. It is not a card transaction |

:::note Only an applied surcharge prints a fee line
Admin fee and dual pricing print no fee line until a business rule changes this. Their fee is
included in the amount line, so the amount, the tip, the tax and the fee lines always add up to the
total. This is deliberate, not an omission. Do not add a fee line for either program on your own.
:::

See [Receipt Compliance](receipt-compliance.md) for the fields that every Handpoint receipt carries.

## Compliance for a surcharge

:::warning These obligations belong to the merchant
Neither the SDK nor the terminal can discharge them. Confirm them with the merchant before you
enable a surcharge.
:::

- The merchant tells the cardholder **before** the transaction. Signage at the entrance, and at the
  point of sale.
- The merchant gives the card networks and the acquirer **30 days notice** before the first
  surcharge.
- The maximum is **3%** of the transaction, or the actual cost of acceptance, whichever is lower.
- A debit card or a prepaid card never carries a surcharge, even when the customer selects
  "credit" at the terminal.
- Several US states and Puerto Rico prohibit a surcharge.

## Reporting

Every fee is recorded, so a merchant can answer these questions:

- How much did I collect in fees this month, and under which program?
- On this transaction, did the platform charge a fee? If it did not, why not?
- Which of my terminals run which program?
- Can I export all of it?

Four values reach the transaction list and the exported report: the fee amount, the program, whether
the fee applied, and the tax attributed to the fee.

A transaction with no fee shows empty values, never `0.00`. A fee of zero and no fee at all are
different facts.

:::note Reporting is still in development
The four values are not yet in the merchant reports or in the
[Transaction Feed API](../back-office/transaction-feed-api.md). Persist the fee result in your own
records until they arrive.
:::

## Scope

**Supported:**

- Card transactions.
- Surcharge, admin fee and dual pricing.
- The Android SDK on PAX terminals.

**Not supported:**

| Item | Reason |
|---|---|
| Cash and e-gift tenders | The gateway processes card transactions. Your point of sale handles cash |
| Cash discount at the gateway | It applies to a cash tender, which never reaches the gateway |
| The Cloud API | Coming soon. The terminal accepts a `fee` object, but the Cloud API does not send one yet |
| The iOS, Windows and Cordova SDKs | The Android SDK carries the feature |
| A recalculation of the fee on a partial refund | The platform does not calculate it. You do |
| Routing that depends on the fee | Out of scope |
| Rules for each BIN or each card brand | Out of scope |

## Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Fee | The money the merchant adds, under any of the four programs |
| Program | Which of the four rules applies |
| Applied | The gateway charged the fee |
| Dropped | The gateway removed the fee, and the transaction continued |
| Refused | The gateway rejected the request, and the transaction stopped |
